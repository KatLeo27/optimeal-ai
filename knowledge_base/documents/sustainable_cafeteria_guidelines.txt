# Sustainable Cafeteria Operations & Measurement Guidelines

## Waste Stream Categorization in Food Service
Institutional waste generally originates in three distinct stages:
1. **Pre-Consumer Prep Waste**: Trimmings, peels, spoilage during storage, and overproduction in the prep kitchen. Target: < 4% of total ingredients.
2. **Overproduction / Unserved Surplus Waste**: Food prepared and displayed on serving lines but never served or sold to patrons. Target: < 3% of total meals prepared.
3. **Post-Consumer Plate Waste**: Leftovers left uneaten by patrons on dining plates. Target: < 30-50 grams per patron meal.

## Financial & Environmental Cost Analysis
- Food waste costs include not only raw ingredient procurement costs, but also embodied labor, energy, water, and waste-hauling disposal fees.
- True Cost of Food Waste = Ingredient Purchase Cost + Kitchen Labor Cost (prep time) + Utility Energy (cooking/refrigeration) + Municipal Disposal Surcharge.
- On average, every $1 invested in institutional food waste reduction delivers approximately $7 in operational savings.

## Practical Steps for Kitchen Managers
1. Conduct daily end-of-shift waste weigh-ins by meal section (e.g., Soups, Vegetables, Meats, Starches).
2. Review weekly anomaly alerts to identify dishes with persistent consumer rejection or high surplus rates.
3. Establish a standard operating procedure (SOP) for safe rapid chilling and donation to local food rescue partners.
